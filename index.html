Problem

Sell white label software to buyers who do not know INZINT, in a crowded market, and close deals that need custom scope, not a standard price list.

What I did

  • Owned full cycle deals: prospecting, qualification, proposals, and close.
  • Created custom proposals and SOWs for each client, instead of sending a generic deck.
  • Passed client insights back to internal teams to guide feature improvements.
  • Used outbound, LinkedIn and email, plus CRM discipline, to keep pipeline moving across multiple deals at once.

Tools used

Full cycle B2B salesProposalsSOWsCRMLinkedIn outreachEmail outreach

Result

$500K+ in new revenue closed, across 7 clients, with project sizes typically $35K to $45K plus dev staffing work. That result is from my own career record, not a client testimonial, and no client names are published here.

What I learned

Custom scope wins over generic pitching, and client insight is a product input, not just a sales note. That lesson carries into how I build products now: start from a real buyer problem, write the scope clearly, and iterate from what buyers actually say.

Informational guides and prompts only. They do not send emails, make calls, build lists, or book meetings for you. You still do the outreach yourself.

All 20 plays

  1. Cap your research at ten minutes and land one usable line

  4. Source senior buyer messaging from filings and earnings calls
  5. Mine your own recorded calls for your persona's exact language
  6. Build one messaging matrix per persona

  8. Pitch a problem proposition, not a value proposition
  9. Make the first line of every cold email entirely about the prospect
  10. Write signal emails in four sentences
  11. Open cold calls with a tailored permission opener
  12. Send short personalised videos as a sequence touch
  13. Batch your prospecting account by account
  14. Personalise at scale with AI, checked by a human before sending
  15. Replace the meeting ask with a tiered offer or an asset
  16. Offer the blind date: sell an insight, not your product
  17. Defuse prospecting stage objections with a named framework
  18. Defuse "we already have a solution"
  19. Answer "send me some information" without losing the conversation

Cold Email: 20 Outbound Plays You Can Hand to Your Agent

For a founder, sales rep, or small sales team doing outbound by email. Every play works on its own. Together they form one system: a subject line that earns the open, a first line about the prospect, a short body built around one problem, and a close that is easy to say yes to, followed by bumps and a breakup that close the sequence cleanly.

20 plays, each with a copy paste agent version. Informational guides and prompts only. They do not send emails, make calls, build lists, or book meetings for you. You still do the outreach yourself.

All 20 plays

  1. Write subject lines that look like internal email
  2. Use bumps and a breakup to close the sequence
  3. Keep cold email short, plain-text and mobile-first
  4. Write cold email with the REPLY method
  5. Open with a real observation, not a compliment or a merge tag
  6. Write executive cold emails with the Problem Prompter: one idea, unsure tone, peer proof, soft close
  7. Write the executive prospecting email line by line: objective, unsure problem, peer story, idea CTA, PS personalization
  8. Score cold emails on the five data backed elements
  9. Make the CTA something easy to say yes to, like samples
  10. Write cold email around pain, risk and offer, with trained personalisation
  11. Use email to reinforce the call, personalised to one or two points
  12. Keep first cold email short and mobile-readable
  13. Fit executive cold email to a nine-second scan
  14. Offer a peer insight instead of asking an executive for a meeting
  15. Use an interest call to action on cold email, a specific one in-deal
  16. Write cold emails in a four-part reason, problem, value, CTA framework
  17. Use the three-tier offer system to engage the 97% who are not in market
  18. Replace spray and pray templates with a few precise emails
  19. Keep cold email concise and 75% about their problem, 25% about you
  20. Cut cold emails to 75-120 words by deleting preamble and sourcing

Multichannel Sequences: 20 Outbound Plays You Can Hand to Your Agent

For a founder, sales rep, or small sales team doing outbound across email, phone, voicemail and LinkedIn. Every play works on its own. Together they form one system: one problem, carried across channels, with each touch pointing back to the last one, instead of five separate messages about five separate things.

20 plays, each with a copy paste agent version. Informational guides and prompts only. They do not send emails, make calls, build lists, or book meetings for you. You still do the outreach yourself.

All 20 plays

  1. Leave voicemails that point back to the email
  2. Build the sequence in problem chunks across channels
  3. Tier sequences by account value: high-touch for A accounts, automated for the rest
  4. Sequence six messages in two problem chunks with channels driving back to email
  5. Batch the whole prospecting cycle weekly, then execute a 12-step persona flow
  6. Go phone first: most meetings come from calls, not email
  7. Run a true omnichannel sequence instead of relying on one channel
  8. Structure sequences as value sets, not disjointed follow-ups
  9. Weave cadence steps together across channels
  10. Run phone-first, three-channel cadences in three to five touches
  11. Run four to seven touches with a new angle each time
  12. Treat step three as the meeting email and switch channels after it
  13. Rebalance from AI email volume toward human conversation
  14. Run separate base sequences above and below the power line, plus purpose-built campaign sequences
  15. Call prospects who send negative email replies instead of emailing back
  16. Prospect in multichannel bursts instead of a predictable weekly drip
  17. Tag-team accounts between AE and SDR so the same message lands from a second sender
  18. Use the three-strikes callback to bank future meetings
  19. Three strikes, then book the 30-day follow-up
  20. Run the triple: never make a naked dial

ICP and Targeting: 20 Outbound Plays You Can Hand to Your Agent

For a founder, sales rep, or small sales team doing outbound who needs to pick the right accounts and the right people inside them before writing a single email. Every play works on its own. Together they form one system: learn from your best customers, narrow the list, tier it, map the personas, and disqualify everything outside the slot.

20 plays, each with a copy paste agent version. Informational guides and prompts only. They do not send emails, make calls, build lists, or book meetings for you. You still do the outreach yourself.

All 20 plays

  1. Build the ICP from best customers and closed-won lookalikes
  2. Tier accounts and match outreach effort to the tier
  3. Map the golden path of personas before prospecting
  4. Define incision points first and build one sequence per persona from its top two problems
  5. Define your slot, close-lost everything outside it, and ban out-of-slot meetings
  6. Research an executive account in four ingredients before writing any outreach
  7. Write a different talk track per persona, weighted to company relevance
  8. Prioritise with the hierarchy of relevance and the martini glass
  9. Shrink outbound to a martini glass: fewer verticals and personas, much deeper
  10. Give reps a prioritised account focus instead of letting them work a list top to bottom
  11. Reverse-engineer your last wins to build a martini-glass territory plan
  12. Enter through a narrow wedge, then expand
  13. Map TAM and prioritise ICPs in tiers before campaigning
  14. Scale pre-call research in proportion to deal size
  15. Run micro-segments of fifty or fewer with two personal attributes
  16. Tier every account A, B or C on use cases plus likelihood of budget, then have reps validate the data
  17. Build high-velocity pipeline from inbound plus a named target account list
  18. Run extreme firmographic sprints to make reps industry experts
  19. Tier a new territory green, yellow and red by size of prize times likelihood to close
  20. Set no-exception rules of engagement for who can work which accounts
